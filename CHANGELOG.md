## Unreleased — Wi-Fi recovery can no longer freeze the Tab5; retries every 30 s; Connect always tries now — 2026-11-22

Found the morning after the entry below: overnight on the hub (no cables touched) the C6 kept resetting, and from 04:21, with the battery down to about 35%, it browned out even at 11 dBm, during its own boot. At 04:57 a recovery called `esp_hosted_deinit()` from the main loop and it never returned. A debugger attach (OpenOCD on COM17's USB JTAG, all task stacks) showed why: the C6 had died mid-boot and never sent its transport INIT, ESP-Hosted's `sdio_write` task waits for that INIT in a loop that does not check for shutdown (`eh_host_bus_sdio.c`, `sdio_write_task`), and deinit joins that task with no timeout. Everything else was idle; the radio had been paused for the recovery and was never resumed. Fixed:

- **Teardown with a time limit** (`wifi_service.cpp`). `esp_hosted_deinit()` runs in a helper task (PSRAM stack); the main loop waits at most 5 s. On a timeout the log says `RTL_WIFI_LINK_WEDGED reason=hosted_deinit_timeout`, the Wi-Fi status reads "Wi-Fi stuck - restart the Tab5", the screen and receiver keep running, and later recoveries return at once instead of blocking again (the stuck helper keeps ESP-Hosted's lock, so Wi-Fi needs a restart). The ESP-Hosted bug itself is upstream; `managed_components` is not in git, so it is not patched here.
- **Retries every 30 s, no waits that grow** (owner's rule). The first recovery runs 2 s after the loss, every later one 30 s after the last, for as long as the link is down. This replaces the 2 / 10 / 30 s spacing and the 5, 10, 20, 30-minute slow retries from the entry below: plugging a second device into the hub (08:13) took Wi-Fi down for 5 minutes because the boot had spent the quick budget. Each recovery pauses the radio briefly.
- **Review (Grok, PR 261):** the wedged-teardown log line now shows the wait it actually did (0 ms when an earlier teardown is still stuck, not 5000); stale comments about growing retry pauses and about `sd_writer` never blocking were corrected (it writes inline, as before, if its task cannot be created). The other findings in the Wi-Fi, sign-in/transfer and background-save slices were checked against the code: none was both real and new; the pre-existing ones are issues 263 and 264.
- **A connection that holds 20 s earns back one quick recovery** (the full budget still comes back after 5 minutes up).
- **Connect on the Connectivity page always tries now** (owner's rule; new network or saved one). It clears the retry wait and counts and, when the C6 link is down, runs the recovery at once; the connect follows it (`RTL_WIFI_USER_CONNECT recovery_now=0|1`).
- **Host tests:** `wifi_supervisor` (30 s spacing, earned recovery once per connection, the 31 s-then-blip case, Connect during a retry wait, with the link failed, with the link up). Full host suite passes.
- **Device (COM17):** the bounded teardown flashed 08:11; every teardown since finished at once (`esp_hosted_deinit=0`), none timed out, so the timeout path has not run on the device. A boot on the hub with the second device attached reached Wi-Fi in 80 s (11 dBm, C6 power-cycled); a replug at 08:13 dropped it and the old slow retry restored it 08:18. Final build flashed 08:26 with the interference source unplugged: Wi-Fi 19 s after boot. **Not yet seen on the device:** the 30 s retries, the Connect override, the earned recovery.

## Unreleased — Wi-Fi rides through bad USB power: C6 brown-outs recover in seconds — 2026-11-22

Root cause, measured with the C6's own console (adapter on its TX pin) next to the P4 log: with the Tab5's USB-C on a powered hub that has other devices attached, the hub's 5 V most likely dips when those devices draw current (the dip itself was not measured). The USB-C cable powers the Tab5 and carries its serial data, but on that hub it did not cover the whole load: the battery fell from 100% to 34% overnight while connected, never charging (corrected 2026-11-22; an earlier version of this entry said the Tab5 runs from USB and not the battery whenever USB-C is present, which the overnight drain contradicts). The C6 is the part that falls over: during its transmit bursts its log shows "E BOD: Brownout detector was triggered" or rst:0x1 (POWERON), while a diagnostic build showed its reset pin (GPIO 15) and its power switch (WLAN_PWR_EN) did not move. Battery only, or a direct PC port: no fault. Our firmware then turned each blip into a long outage. Fixed:

- **Recover without power-cycling the C6 first.** The first recovery of a series re-initialises the link only (`RTL_WIFI_LINK_RECOVERY result=ok c6_power_cycle=0`); later tries still power-cycle a C6 that is truly wedged. Power-cycling a C6 that has just restarted added another inrush on the same supply.
- **Adaptive transmit-power cap (`c6_tx_policy`, new, host-tested).** Full power normally (the C6 is not touched unless a cap was ever set). Each C6 link failure (at most once per 30 s) steps the cap down: 20 dBm (C6 default) -> 15 -> 11 dBm; after 10 minutes of stable link it steps back up one level. **Back-off:** a failure within the current wait after a step up (seen on the hub: the C6 reset 12 s after the cap returned to full power) doubles the wait before the next step up (10, 20, 40 minutes ... at most 2 hours), so a supply that stays bad settles at the level that holds instead of dropping the link every 10 minutes; 2 hours stable at full power resets the wait to 10 minutes. The cap is stored at the failure and applied when the link is re-initialised (`RTL_WIFI_TXPWR cap_qdbm=... readback_qdbm=...`, `RTL_WIFI_C6_TX_CAP level=... why=link_failure|stable_step_up`). The C6 firmware in use reports its reset reason as 0 in its INIT event, so the link failure, not the reason, is the trigger; the reason is still read (`c6_last_reset_reason()`).
- **Hold the OrcDial bridge 15 s after the C6 link comes up** (`RTL_ORCDIAL_BRIDGE_HOLD`). The bridge's first broadcast is a large transmit burst; on the hub the C6 browned out 30 to 50 ms after `ORCDIAL_V4_BRIDGE_READY`, every time.
- **Space the quick recoveries 2 s / 10 s / 30 s** (were 2 s each), so a burst of supply noise cannot spend all three within seconds.
- **A failed connect request schedules a retry** (it used to fall through with nothing scheduled; Wi-Fi sat idle for 80 s once), and **a C6 that does not answer a connect request (ESP_FAIL / timeout) marks the link failed at once** (`RTL_WIFI_LINK_LOST reason=c6_no_response`), so recovery starts in about 2 s.
- **Device (COM17, M5Stack Tab5, on the hub, second device attached):** a C6 reset at full power (00:26:58) -> link loss detected 00:27:03.8 -> recovery 00:27:05.9 with the cap stepped to 15 dBm -> link re-initialised 0.7 s later without a power-cycle (C6 readback 60) -> router rejected the first reconnect (reason 2) -> Wi-Fi connected 00:27:30, 32 s after the reset, no user action. A boot brown-out recovered the same way (Wi-Fi 8.4 s after recovery started). After 10 minutes stable the cap stepped back to full power (C6 readback 80). Back-off on the device: step up to full power at 00:59:24, C6 power-on reset 73 s later (01:00:37), recovered at 15 dBm without a power-cycle, Wi-Fi connected 01:01:10; the next step up came at 01:21:10, 20 minutes stable instead of 10. Full power failed again 18 s later (back at 01:22:00, 15 dBm), and the next step up came 40 minutes later (02:02:00); it failed after 54 s and Wi-Fi was back at 02:03:31 (37 s, no power-cycle), with the next try at 80 minutes. On this hub full power never held (3 of 3) while 15 dBm held for 20 and 40 minutes; every outage recovered by itself in 32 to 37 s. At a fixed 11 dBm, four hub replugs gave no C6 brown-out at all. Before these fixes the same event spent all three recoveries in about 12 s and left Wi-Fi down for 5 minutes (until a reboot before 21654d83).
- **Not fixed (hardware):** the dip itself; the C6's supply has too little headroom for its transmit current on that input. **Not checked:** range at 15 / 11 dBm over distance; the boot-time USB-A power-cycle of the RTL-SDR (`BOOT_RTL_RECOVERY`) can also reset the C6 through the dongle's inrush (seen once on the hub, not yet handled).

## Unreleased — Wi-Fi no longer gives up for good after three link losses — 2026-11-21

Found during the device tests: Wi-Fi went down and stayed down until a reboot. When the C6 link fails, the firmware recovers it (at most three times, the budget refilling only after 5 minutes connected); after the third failure it set "Wi-Fi link lost - restart Wi-Fi" and never tried again. At boot all three recoveries can be spent within a minute. Now, once the budget is spent, `wifi_supervisor` schedules one more recovery after a long pause that grows (5, 10, 20, then every 30 minutes; the status reads "Wi-Fi link lost - retrying in N min" and the console `RTL_WIFI_LINK_RETRY_SCHEDULED retry=N delay_s=S`). Each slow retry is one attempt, not a fresh budget, so a C6 that keeps failing still cannot loop; the series restarts after 5 minutes connected. A slow retry that comes due while Wi-Fi is already back is skipped (`RTL_WIFI_LINK_RETRY_SKIPPED reason=connected`) so a working link is never torn down. The quick recoveries and the opt-in remote keep-alive are unchanged.

- **What triggers the link losses (measured, not fixed):** on COM17 (M5Stack Tab5, Nooelec Smart v5 dongle) every link loss after the splash came within 0.05 to 1.2 s of the RTL-SDR dropping off USB (`usb disconnected`, then `SDIO ... 0x107`, `unrecoverable write failure` or `no slave credits`): 4 of 4, 1 of 1, 3 of 3 and 3 of 3 in four runs; a 4-minute run with no USB drop had no link loss. The same happens on the firmware from before the refactor (96a5b8a0), so it is not a refactor regression. It matches issues 109 (a USB drop knocks out the C6) and 244 (the C6 stops answering). Separately, two boots lost the link during the first connect in the splash, before USB starts. Which band was restored did not decide it (P25 and Shortwave boots both failed and both succeeded).
- **Host tests:** slow retry scheduled after the budget is spent (5 min), the next one 10 min, a recovery that left the station down also retried, nothing scheduled while the link is up, a due retry skipped when connected, the pause series (5, 10, 20, 30, 30 min) and its reset after 5 minutes connected. Full host suite passes.
- **Device:** a boot that spent its three recoveries by 77 s printed `RTL_WIFI_LINK_RETRY_SCHEDULED retry=1 delay_s=300`; at 5 minutes the retry ran and Wi-Fi connected again; when the dongle dropped off USB again a minute later, the next retry was scheduled for 10 minutes.
- **Not fixed:** why the dongle drops off USB (cable, connector or power) and the C6 stall it causes.
- **Trigger found later the same day:** the PC USB port the Tab5's USB-C cable was plugged into. With that port, every boot lost the C6 link about 1 s after the RTL-SDR started streaming or the OrcDial bridge started (and the dongle dropped off USB), on this branch, on a local build of v0.6.0 and on the published v0.6.0 release app (SHA-256 checked) alike; on battery with no PC cable everything worked. Ruled out on the device: the OrcDial bridge (a build without it still failed), the charger (a build with charging off still failed), console output (QUIET verbosity still failed), the dongle (two different dongles failed) and the C6 being switched off by the I/O-expander code (M5Unified changes single bits). Moving the same cable to another PC USB port: two 3-minute boots, Wi-Fi connected, 0 C6 failures, 0 USB drops, streaming and bridge running. Not measured: what is different about the first port.

## Unreleased — Shortwave and P25 touch quirks fixed (owner rulings) — 2026-11-21

Three quirks recorded when the Shortwave and P25 touch handlers left `main.cpp`; the owner ruled on each.

- **Shortwave: a refused retune is no longer remembered as the tuned frequency.** `tune_hz`, `step_up` and `step_down` noted the new frequency and then ignored whether the receiver accepted the retune. The frequency on the screen was never wrong (it follows the receiver), but the noted frequency is the one Shortwave reopens on, so a refused retune could make the next visit open on a frequency the receiver had refused. Now, when the retune or listen request is refused, the noted frequency is set back to where the receiver really is, and the dashboard prints `RTL_SHORTWAVE_TUNE_REFUSED frequency_hz=<asked> live=0|1 receiver_hz=<actual>`. Accepted retunes are unchanged, call for call. The two hooks now return the receiver's answer.
- **P25: SKIP with no talkgroup known says so.** SKIP still drops a followed call as before, but when the talkgroup is not yet decoded nothing can be remembered and the same talkgroup can pull the receiver straight back. It now prints `RTL_P25_SKIP tg=unknown remembered=0 left_call=0|1` so a "skip did nothing" moment can be explained.
- **Shortwave: leaving to Settings or Home now refreshes the screen**, like the P25 and AM handlers. Shortwave used to return without the refresh; the owner has seen stale screens around Shortwave. (Settings owns its own drawing, so the refresh does nothing there; on Home it redraws Home's values.)
- **Host tests:** new cases for a refused live retune and a refused listen, for SKIP with no talkgroup (with and without a followed call), and the two Shortwave exits now expect the refresh. Full host suite passes.
- **Device (COM17, M5Stack Tab5):** opened Shortwave and left through the header to Settings and to Home: both screenshots fully drawn (Home with its live waterfall). P25 SKIP on the control channel printed `RTL_P25_SKIP tg=unknown remembered=0 left_call=0`. Boot with no self-check failures, 35 of 35 refusals.
- **Not checked:** a refused Shortwave retune on the device (the dongle accepted every frequency tried; covered by the host tests), SKIP during a followed voice call on the device.

## Unreleased — Audio recording buffer: PSRAM only (MEM-C, item 7) — 2026-11-21

Owner chose option 1. The 12 s recording buffer (1,152,000 bytes) used to try PSRAM, then fall back to a general 8-bit allocation. That fallback could not succeed: when PSRAM has no room for 1.15 MB, the rest of the heap (the P4 has 768 KB of internal RAM in total, most of it already in use at boot) has no room either. The fallback and its `alloc_internal` hook are removed; when PSRAM is short the recording refuses with the same `RTL_REC_ERR no_buffer` line as before. No change in practice; the code no longer suggests a fallback that cannot work.

- **Host test:** `test_internal_fallback_kept` replaced by `test_psram_only` (PSRAM short: refuses, nothing else tried, nothing freed; room again: the next start succeeds). Full host suite passes.
- **Device (COM17, M5Stack Tab5):** boot log identical to the 6a/6c firmware (apart from one line interleaved with another task's output), 35 of 35 refusals, `main_stack_hwm` 6,516, `RTL_MEM_BUF audio_rec bytes=0 place=none` at idle as before.
- **Not checked:** a recording on the device, and a recording with PSRAM exhausted (the claim that the fallback could never succeed is arithmetic, not measured).

## Unreleased — setup() split into named boot stages: item 6, steps 6a and 6c — 2026-11-21

No behaviour change intended. Owner chose 6a and 6c only; `loop()` and the early part of `setup()` are left as they were. The 30 module self-checks are now `setup_self_checks()`, which returns false where `setup()` used to `return` (the seven fatal checks: Shortwave x4, CB, Airband, receiver controls); `setup()` does `if (!setup_self_checks()) return;`. Everything from there to Home (splash, settings, SD, Wi-Fi, the RTL-SDR, the splash gate, and the `ORC_LORA_TEST_BUILD` alternative boot) is `setup_boot_to_home()`, which keeps the final `rtl_boot_complete` store so the LoRa test branch's early `return` still skips it as before. Both moved by script and checked token for token; both `noinline`. `setup()` is now about 75 lines, ending in two calls.

**New dashboard (AIS, the 20th All Dashboards entry).** Receives AIS on both channels (161.975 and 162.025 MHz) from one 162.000 MHz / 240 kS/s stream, from the USB dongle or an rtl_tcp server, and shows vessels, base stations and aids to navigation on seven pages. Branch `claude/ais-integration-hardware-79f921`; checkpoint in [docs/AIS_INTEGRATION_CHECKPOINT.md](docs/AIS_INTEGRATION_CHECKPOINT.md), evidence in [docs/evidence/ais/2026-11-10-tab5-replay.md](docs/evidence/ais/2026-11-10-tab5-replay.md).

- **Proven on the Tab5 (COM17):** deterministic synthetic AIS IQ replayed over a private rtl_tcp server decodes 43 of 43 transmitted packets byte for byte with none missing or extra, on both channels, with no lost samples. All seven pages render. Names, call signs, destinations, navigation status, base stations and aids to navigation appear. USB and rtl_tcp sources both reach `receiving`, and switching between them works. Fifteen open/close cycles show no PSRAM leak.
- **Not proven:** reception of real ships (the lab saw only noise), sensitivity, sustained USB operation, comparison with another decoder on the same real IQ, regression of FM, FT8 and ADS-B after this change. Replay is network-only and every page says "TEST REPLAY DATA" while it runs.
- **Receiver:** per-burst carrier-offset tracking (strong bursts decode at up to 4 kHz offset in the host stress test, was 1.25 kHz). About 32% of one core on the Tab5 at 240 kS/s.
- **Decoded fields:** Types 1-3, 4, 5, 9, 18, 19, 21, 24, 27, 28 carry fields; the rest are header only. This is not 28 complete decoders.
- **Memory:** about 70 KB decoder state and 17 KB snapshot in PSRAM while the dashboard is open, freed on exit. Binary 5,585,680 bytes (11% of the app partition free).
- **Safety:** the dashboard never shows synthetic data on its own; `RTL_AIS TEST ON` (authenticated) only labels replayed data.
- **Serial:** `RTL_UI OPEN AIS`, `RTL_AIS STATUS`, `RTL_AIS TEST ON|OFF`, `RTL_AIS BENCH`.

## Unreleased — rtl_tcp remote receiver — 2026-11-09

**New receiver source.** The Tab5 can take its IQ from a standard `rtl_tcp` server on the network (for example a Raspberry Pi with an RTL-SDR) instead of a dongle on its own USB port, and can serve its own dongle with an `rtl_tcp` server. Branch `claude/orcsdr-rtl-tcp-interop-f12b5d`; full test record in [docs/RTL_TCP_RELEASE_TEST_PLAN.md](docs/RTL_TCP_RELEASE_TEST_PLAN.md).

- **Client:** standard protocol (12-byte greeting, 5-byte commands, raw 8-bit IQ). Link-adaptive sample rate (1.44, 0.96, 0.24 MS/s ladder; LoRa, POCSAG and P25 stay at 960 kS/s because their decoders need it, and the sample-rate cap does not apply to them), reconnects forever (fast attempts, then every 30 s), names a server whose single client slot is taken ("server busy or silent") and waits up to two minutes for it, and absorbs short DSP stalls instead of dropping samples.
- **Remote capabilities:** the server's greeting (tuner type, gain steps) now drives the gain controls, so manual and auto gain work with no local dongle plugged in. Only what the protocol can carry is offered; the bias tee is never sent to a remote server.
- **Settings > RTL_TCP:** one page for every rtl_tcp setting (source, server, sample-rate cap, frequency correction, direct sampling, offset tuning) with live connection, server and traffic status. The Settings page selector now scrolls.
- **Fixes found while testing:** FT8 and Shortwave SSB read the idle local driver's centre frequency on a remote stream (FT8 decoded nothing, SSB could be up to 6 kHz off); one sample gap made FT8 skip every later slot; an internal RAM leak on each FT8 visit. A band change on a remote stream mis-tuned the FT8 and SSB passband by up to 5 kHz (FT8 decoded nothing after any band button); switching back to AUTO gain could leave the server at an old manual gain. A Wi-Fi outage longer than about four minutes used to leave the Tab5 offline for good (reconnect and C6 recovery are bounded), and a Wi-Fi scan could fail with "radio pause failed" when a remote stream had nothing to start; both are fixed. A server name that does not resolve is reported as "name not found", and a burst of retunes can no longer lose the last one. After a Wi-Fi recovery the remote stream stayed idle (the resume request was dropped); it now restarts. A repeatable outage test (`tools/rtl_tcp_wifi_outage_test.py`, firmware command `RTL_WIFI_OUTAGE`) needs no router. A new Settings > RTL_TCP toggle, KEEP WI-FI ALIVE (on by default, acts only while a remote receiver is selected), re-arms Wi-Fi reconnect and C6 link recovery every 5 minutes while Wi-Fi stays down (docs/RTL_TCP_WIFI_RECOVERY.md); a once-a-minute `RTL_WIFI_HEALTH` log line records the Wi-Fi state. Remote ADS-B names its bandwidth need (about 4.1 MB/s) on screen. The `rtl_tcp` server could be asked to bind `0.0.0.0` (all interfaces) if Wi-Fi reported no address yet; it now refuses any all-interfaces spelling. A new stream resets frequency correction, offset tuning and direct sampling, which a stock server keeps from the previous client.
- **Wi-Fi throughput (Tab5 build):** lwIP pinned to core 1, 256 KB TCP window with scaling, larger lwIP mailboxes, SDIO queues TX 16 / RX 8 with 4 receive staging slots, SDIO clock 20 MHz. Raw TCP receive rose from about 1.8 MB/s to about 3.5 MB/s (28 Mbit/s sustained, `RTL_TCP BENCH`). The larger SDIO transmit queue costs internal DMA RAM (low-water about 20 KB measured). A matching ESP32-C6 Wi-Fi profile (`orcdial/c6/sdkconfig.defaults`: more Wi-Fi buffers, A-MPDU windows 32) takes effect only after the C6 is updated (`RTL_ORCDIAL_C6_UPDATE CONFIRM`); an app flash alone does not apply it. ESP-Hosted 3.1.0 patch variants are included for a future upgrade; the pinned component is still 3.0.6.
- **Over the internet:** the stream was measured across a real cellular hop and decodes (synthetic SSTV frame, 98-101% link). A receive-queue size that was a third of what the TCP window needs made the Tab5 lose data when a server sent large bursts (the stock rtl_tcp sends 256 KB at a time); it is now sized to the window (256 KB bursts: 76% -> 99%).
- **Known gain on connect:** rtl_tcp keeps its tuner gain between clients, so a new stream used to inherit whatever the last client or dashboard left (FT8 about 28 dB too low while showing AUTO). Every remote stream for a new band now starts from tuner AGC.
- **Serial control:** every `RTL_TCP` / `RTL_TCPD` command except `STATUS` needs an authenticated session (extra spaces no longer slip `RTL_TCPD ENABLE` past the check).
- **Server:** off by default, binds only to a named LAN address, never all interfaces. `rtl_tcp` has no authentication; do not expose port 1234 to the internet.
- **Verified on the Tab5 against a Pi 4B running stock RTL-SDR Blog `rtl_tcp` 1.4.0:** all 19 dashboards open on the remote stream; one-hour soak; FT8 decodes (7-16 messages per slot) with and without a local dongle; remote gain changes the measured input level; recovery after a 2-minute server outage. **Not yet verified:** ACARS, SSTV and ADS-B decoding on a remote stream, frequency correction, direct sampling and offset tuning on a real signal, the gain popups on every dashboard, and audio judged by ear, and a server on a public address with no relay. The stock `rtl_tcp` on a Pi was also measured across a real cellular hop through an SSH relay: live FT8, 194 decodes in 31 slots over 8 minutes at 100% link. Changing the receiver source applies at the next stream start, not immediately.
