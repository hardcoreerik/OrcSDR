## Unreleased — PlutoSDR as a local receiver in the Tab5 app — 2026-11-12

- **New receiver class:** a PlutoSDR (AD9361, 70 MHz to 6 GHz) on the Tab5's USB-A port is used like an RTL-SDR dongle. `ui/pluto_source.cpp` adapts the standalone esp-pluto-sdr driver (pinned at `ca88e37`, includes the libiio `CLOSE_PIPE` fix) and feeds the same IQ ring the dongle and rtl_tcp use, so the existing demodulators and screens are shared. Source order is dongle, then Pluto, then remote rtl_tcp (`receiver_source_policy`, host-tested). Gain goes through `rtl_source_routing`. Receive only; the app never writes a transmit setting.
- **Proven on the Tab5 (COM39):** the Pluto enumerates, the stream starts at 0.96 and 2.4 MS/s, and FM audio plays (owner listened).
- **Known problem, not fixed:** intermittent boot-time crash. 3 of 4 boots in a 30-minute capture hit `assert failed: usb_dwc_hal_chan_decode_intr (CHHLTD)` in the USB host interrupt, each right after the FM auto-gain wrote a new gain to the Pluto about 1 s after stream start; the device recovers on its own after the reboot. Cause not confirmed. Do not treat the Pluto source as release quality.
- **Update 2026-11-13 — replug and recovery:**
  - A replugged or re-enumerated Pluto now restarts its stream (the hot-plug resume only accepted an RTL dongle before). Proven on COM39: `PLUTO_ATTACHED`, `RTL_HOTPLUG_RESUME`, `RTL_START ESP_OK`; a retune to 96.1 MHz took.
  - The driver pin is now the queued-reads branch (`383cd73`, pipelined bulk IN reads, not yet merged to esp-pluto-sdr main). Ten reboots with that driver and the diagnostics showed 0 USB asserts against a 2 of 10 baseline; the same ten-reboot run on the final configuration is not done, so the crash is not shown fixed.
  - Recovery ladder in `ui/pluto_source.cpp`: failed starts or a previously used Pluto that does not return trigger a power cycle of the USB-A 5 V rail (hook `on_pluto_power_cycle` in `main.cpp`), with backoff of 45 s, 2, 5 and 10 minutes, at most 6 tries, then it reports that the board needs a manual power cycle. A small NVS flag remembers that a Pluto has been used and is cleared when the ladder gives up. The IDF host library has no per-device reset, so the rail is the only real reset.
  - Seen once on COM39: after a Tab5 restart the Pluto did not enumerate, the ladder cycled the rail about 30 s into the boot, the Pluto attached at 61 s and streamed with no cable touched. Whether the Pluto was wedged or only still booting in that run is not known (see the correction below). The cycle needs the Pluto powered only from the Tab5; a PC cable on its debug port keeps it alive and defeats the cycle.
  - Soak support: the Pluto source prints `PLUTO_HEALTH present= running= alive= state= samples= src_drops= usb_err= ring_drops= cycles= int_free= dma_largest= psram_free=` once a minute. `tools/pluto_soak.py` logs the Tab5 serial and, from the PC, pings the Pluto every 10 s and runs a read-only ssh probe every 5 minutes; it never resets the Tab5 or writes to either device. An 8 hour soak started 2026-11-14 on COM39 (results in `F:\Ai\_pluto_soak\run1_2026-11-14`, not in the repo). At start `dma_largest` was 5120 bytes, the scarce resource to watch.
  - Low-battery WARNING for the host (2026-11-14, redesigned twice after owner feedback; it is a warning only and can never stop, refuse or delay anything): the host battery is read once a minute (the same read as the health line, no extra polling) and a level only counts when sustained across readings: discharging at or below 20% for 2 readings in a row is "low", at or below 10% for 3 is "critical", one good reading resets the count, and it clears when charging or back at 25%. The only effects are a log line and the Home header chip text `HOST BATT` (no colour change). A reading that cannot be trusted (no cell above 2 V, a battery swapped on external power, a failed read) clears the warning and restarts the count. The host cannot see how the Pluto is powered (its second port, a PC cable, Ethernet), so the warning is about the host battery only and claims nothing about the Pluto. An earlier version could stop the stream in an opt-in mode; it was removed because bench conditions change (batteries swapped, power supplies added) and no battery reading may switch a function off. Switch: NVS `orcpluto`/`guard` = 0 turns the warning off. Code: `ui/pluto_power_guard.hpp` (pure logic, host test `tests/pluto_power_guard_host_test.cpp` in `tools/test-rtl-tcp.sh` and CI, also under the sanitizers, including a battery-swap scenario), `ui/host_power.*` (the read and the no-battery rule), `ui/pluto_home.*` (the PLUTO tag, the rate and the warning on Home); `main.cpp` has only a one-line call. The earlier stop/resume logic was verified on COM39 with simulated readings; this warning-only version is host-tested and builds, and is verified on the device only as far as the shared read and chip path. Not verified: a real battery run-down, and a battery-less Tab5.
  - Driver repinned (2026-11-14) to esp-pluto-sdr `dd5fd415` (main after PR 4: pipelined bulk IN reads plus the CodeRabbit fixes for init unwinding, a second open, and keeping the device handle until close completes). Verified the fetched source holds the fixes and none of the diagnostic lines. Reboot check on COM39: 11 of 11 boots with 0 `assert failed`, 0 `EP 0 Error`, the Pluto attached and `RTL_START ESP_OK` each time, no boot-time or ladder power cycle. Attach came at 7.2 to 8.1 s, not about 26 s, because the Pluto was also powered from the PC's debug cable and so stayed up through the Tab5 resets (the first time the fast path has been seen); with the Tab5 as its only supply it reboots with each reset and attaches near 26 s. The new close and open paths only matter on failure, which this run did not exercise.
  - Host power and tag (2026-11-14): the once-a-minute `PLUTO_HEALTH` line now also carries `batt_pct batt_mv vbus_mv charging` from the Tab5's power calls (`vbus_mv` reads -1 on this board; charging comes from the PMIC and reads 1 while charging). The Home header chip and the footer's left cell now say `PLUTO` (not `RTL-SDR`) when a Pluto is the local receiver, and the footer rate shows the Pluto's output rate; a dongle or a remote rtl_tcp stream still takes the label first. `tools/pluto_soak.py` records the battery columns, flags a low discharging battery, and gained `--stream-pluto` (the Pluto's journal and telemetry streamed to the PC, reconnecting after a reboot) and `--pluto-diag` (switches the chosen `orc-diag` features on for the run and off again at the end); a lock bug that would have deadlocked the logger on the first stall is fixed. Seen once, shortly after a flash, while taking serial screenshots: the internal DMA headroom fell from 8 KB to about 1 KB and the Pluto failed to start (`no IN completion in time`, `iiod handshake failed`). Not reproduced: a clean reboot with no screenshots held `dma_largest` at 8192 and streamed for 5 minutes (about 144 million samples a minute, battery 77% to 81% and charging), and a single screenshot afterwards changed nothing. Cause unattributed; the Pluto was powered from both the Tab5 and the PC at the time, and several screenshots were taken within about 2 minutes of the boot.
  - `tools/pluto/orc-diag` (Pluto-side, development only, installed on the lab Pluto with every feature off): `on`/`off <feature>` for `journal-sync` (journald flush every 10 s instead of the image's 10 minutes), `watchdog` (systemd arms the Zynq watchdog, 30 s), `telemetry` (10 s log of temperatures, XADC rails, load, memory, USB gadget and Ethernet state, rotated) and `reset-cause` (raw Zynq reset registers at boot, decode not verified). It adds drop-ins and units only (named zz-* so they sort after the image's fishball.conf) and `all-off` restores the stock behaviour. Each switch was turned on and off once on the lab Pluto (effective settings checked each time; the board stayed up 50 s past the 30 s watchdog timeout after disarming) and the board ends with everything off. Not part of the image yet.
  - `tools/pluto_console.py` runs one command on the Pluto's debug UART; the password is typed at a prompt and never stored.
  - Ten-reboot run on the final configuration (COM39, Pluto powered only from the Tab5, 90 s per boot): 9 of 10 boots produced a capture, and in all 9 there were 0 `assert failed`, 0 `EP 0 Error`, the Pluto attached at 39.7 to 40.5 s and `RTL_START ESP_OK` followed. The 10th capture was empty (no serial output at all; cause not found), so this is 0 asserts in 9 observed boots, against a 2 of 10 baseline before the queued-reads driver. Every one of the 9 went through the boot-time rail cut. **Correction (2026-11-14, measured):** this is not a wedged Pluto. A ping timeline through an RTS reset of the Tab5 shows the Pluto going DOWN about 3 s after the reset (the reset drops the USB-A rail; an ssh stream died at the same point) and answering again at 52 s. The Pluto is rebooting, which takes about 25 s, and the boot-time wait (8 s, then 10 s) cuts the rail again mid-boot, restarting that boot. Without that cut it would be up about 15 s sooner. The earlier statement that the gadget does not recover from a host reset is not supported for a Tab5-only-powered Pluto; a Pluto also powered from a PC cable is a separate case that has been seen wedged. Only RTS resets were measured; a panic or software reset may leave the rail alone, which has not been tested.
  - Boot sequence for a remembered Pluto (owner-approved): the boot waits for a dongle or a Pluto; if neither has enumerated after 8 s it cuts the USB-A rail for 3 s (1 s for dongle-only units, which see no change), then waits up to 35 s. The splash says "Detecting receiver…", "Power-cycling the PlutoSDR…" and `Receiver: PlutoSDR ready / waiting for PlutoSDR`, and shows its button once the rail is back on instead of blocking for the whole wait. The recovery ladder holds off for 60 s after a boot-time cycle. `BOOT_SPLASH_SUMMARY` keeps `rtl=` and adds `pluto=`. Proven once on COM39: after a flash reset the Pluto was absent, the 3 s cut brought it up, it attached at 39 s and streamed, no second cycle. The 3 s value and the wait lengths are not validated: see the correction above, the Pluto only needed time to boot. The first wait before the rail cut is now 35 s for a remembered Pluto (owner-approved 2026-11-14; dongle-only units keep 8 s), and the splash shows its button after 8 s. Ten-reboot run on this build (COM39, Tab5-only power, 75 s per boot): 9 captured boots, all with 0 `assert failed`, 0 `EP 0 Error`, no boot-time rail cut, no ladder cycle, the Pluto attached at 25.1 to 26.0 s and `RTL_START ESP_OK` followed. The first capture of the run was empty again (as in the earlier ten-boot run), so it is most likely a harness artifact of the first reset, not shown. Measured once on COM39 after a flash reset: no rail cut, the Pluto attached at 25.8 s (was 42 s with the cut) and the stream started with `RTL_START ESP_OK`; the ping timeline showed the Pluto down at 2.8 s and up at 28.9 s.
- **Not done:** long soak, "RTL-SDR" wording on screens, gain/frequency UI checks, boot sequence for the Pluto, TX.
- **Verified:** full `build-tab5-idf.ps1` build links, 5,616,928 bytes (11% of the app partition free).

## Unreleased — Volume tray redesign, Visualizer entry — 2026-11-11

- **Volume tray:** now 840x280 (3:1, twice the previous size), centred just below the status bar, drawn as the top item: it is composited from a sprite, and the dashboards (scopes, waterfalls, FT8 waterfall) hold still while it is open. The tray reads touch itself; dashboards see no touch until the finger is up and 250 ms have passed, so a tap that opens, adjusts or dismisses the tray never reaches "touch to tune". Only the slider reacts to a held finger; mute fires once per press and the second tap of a double tap on the speaker does not mute.
- **Header:** the VIS button is gone and Home, speaker and Settings are spaced out. The Visualizer is the last entry in All Dashboards (`RTL_UI OPEN VISUALIZER`). `RTL_UI STATUS` now reports `volume`, `audio_enabled` and `tray`.
- **Verified:** host test `tests/audio_header_tray_tests.cpp`; hand-tested by the owner on COM17. `tools/tab5_volume_tray_test.py` (on-device) still shows intermittent failures on a few dashboards that are not yet explained (suspected test timing).
- **Shared code note:** the shared dashboard registry now has a `visualizer` entry and the header geometry changed; P4 Core must treat the entry as unavailable until it has one.

## Unreleased — AIS receiver and dashboard on the Tab5 — 2026-11-10

**New dashboard (AIS, the 20th All Dashboards entry).** Receives AIS on both channels (161.975 and 162.025 MHz) from one 162.000 MHz / 240 kS/s stream, from the USB dongle or an rtl_tcp server, and shows vessels, base stations and aids to navigation on seven pages. Branch `claude/ais-integration-hardware-79f921`; checkpoint in [docs/AIS_INTEGRATION_CHECKPOINT.md](docs/AIS_INTEGRATION_CHECKPOINT.md), evidence in [docs/evidence/ais/2026-11-10-tab5-replay.md](docs/evidence/ais/2026-11-10-tab5-replay.md).

- **Proven on the Tab5 (COM17):** deterministic synthetic AIS IQ replayed over a private rtl_tcp server decodes 43 of 43 transmitted packets byte for byte with none missing or extra, on both channels, with no lost samples. All seven pages render. Names, call signs, destinations, navigation status, base stations and aids to navigation appear. USB and rtl_tcp sources both reach `receiving`, and switching between them works. Fifteen open/close cycles show no PSRAM leak.
- **Not proven:** reception of real ships (the lab saw only noise), sensitivity, sustained USB operation, comparison with another decoder on the same real IQ, regression of FM, FT8 and ADS-B after this change. Replay is network-only and every page says "TEST REPLAY DATA" while it runs.
- **Receiver:** per-burst carrier-offset tracking (strong bursts decode at up to 4 kHz offset in the host stress test, was 1.25 kHz). About 32% of one core on the Tab5 at 240 kS/s.
- **Decoded fields:** Types 1-3, 4, 5, 9, 18, 19, 21, 24, 27, 28 carry fields; the rest are header only. This is not 28 complete decoders.
- **Memory:** about 70 KB decoder state and 17 KB snapshot in PSRAM while the dashboard is open, freed on exit. Binary 5,585,680 bytes (11% of the app partition free).
- **Safety:** the dashboard never shows synthetic data on its own; `RTL_AIS TEST ON` (authenticated) only labels replayed data.
- **Serial:** `RTL_UI OPEN AIS`, `RTL_AIS STATUS`, `RTL_AIS TEST ON|OFF`, `RTL_AIS BENCH`.

## Unreleased — rtl_tcp remote receiver — 2026-11-09

**New receiver source.** The Tab5 can take its IQ from a standard `rtl_tcp` server on the network (for example a Raspberry Pi with an RTL-SDR) instead of a dongle on its own USB port, and can serve its own dongle with an `rtl_tcp` server. Branch `claude/orcsdr-rtl-tcp-interop-f12b5d`; full test record in [docs/RTL_TCP_RELEASE_TEST_PLAN.md](docs/RTL_TCP_RELEASE_TEST_PLAN.md).

- **Client:** standard protocol (12-byte greeting, 5-byte commands, raw 8-bit IQ). Link-adaptive sample rate (1.44, 0.96, 0.24 MS/s ladder; LoRa, POCSAG and P25 stay at 960 kS/s because their decoders need it, and the sample-rate cap does not apply to them), reconnects forever (fast attempts, then every 30 s), names a server whose single client slot is taken ("server busy or silent") and waits up to two minutes for it, and absorbs short DSP stalls instead of dropping samples.
- **Remote capabilities:** the server's greeting (tuner type, gain steps) now drives the gain controls, so manual and auto gain work with no local dongle plugged in. Only what the protocol can carry is offered; the bias tee is never sent to a remote server.
- **Settings > RTL_TCP:** one page for every rtl_tcp setting (source, server, sample-rate cap, frequency correction, direct sampling, offset tuning) with live connection, server and traffic status. The Settings page selector now scrolls.
- **Fixes found while testing:** FT8 and Shortwave SSB read the idle local driver's centre frequency on a remote stream (FT8 decoded nothing, SSB could be up to 6 kHz off); one sample gap made FT8 skip every later slot; an internal RAM leak on each FT8 visit. A band change on a remote stream mis-tuned the FT8 and SSB passband by up to 5 kHz (FT8 decoded nothing after any band button); switching back to AUTO gain could leave the server at an old manual gain. A Wi-Fi outage longer than about four minutes used to leave the Tab5 offline for good (reconnect and C6 recovery are bounded), and a Wi-Fi scan could fail with "radio pause failed" when a remote stream had nothing to start; both are fixed. A server name that does not resolve is reported as "name not found", and a burst of retunes can no longer lose the last one. After a Wi-Fi recovery the remote stream stayed idle (the resume request was dropped); it now restarts. A repeatable outage test (`tools/rtl_tcp_wifi_outage_test.py`, firmware command `RTL_WIFI_OUTAGE`) needs no router. A new Settings > RTL_TCP toggle, KEEP WI-FI ALIVE (on by default, acts only while a remote receiver is selected), re-arms Wi-Fi reconnect and C6 link recovery every 5 minutes while Wi-Fi stays down (docs/RTL_TCP_WIFI_RECOVERY.md); a once-a-minute `RTL_WIFI_HEALTH` log line records the Wi-Fi state. Remote ADS-B names its bandwidth need (about 4.1 MB/s) on screen. The `rtl_tcp` server could be asked to bind `0.0.0.0` (all interfaces) if Wi-Fi reported no address yet; it now refuses any all-interfaces spelling. A new stream resets frequency correction, offset tuning and direct sampling, which a stock server keeps from the previous client.
- **Wi-Fi throughput (Tab5 build):** lwIP pinned to core 1, 256 KB TCP window with scaling, larger lwIP mailboxes, SDIO queues TX 16 / RX 8 with 4 receive staging slots, SDIO clock 20 MHz. Raw TCP receive rose from about 1.8 MB/s to about 3.5 MB/s (28 Mbit/s sustained, `RTL_TCP BENCH`). The larger SDIO transmit queue costs internal DMA RAM (low-water about 20 KB measured). A matching ESP32-C6 Wi-Fi profile (`orcdial/c6/sdkconfig.defaults`: more Wi-Fi buffers, A-MPDU windows 32) takes effect only after the C6 is updated (`RTL_ORCDIAL_C6_UPDATE CONFIRM`); an app flash alone does not apply it. ESP-Hosted 3.1.0 patch variants are included for a future upgrade; the pinned component is still 3.0.6.
- **Over the internet:** the stream was measured across a real cellular hop and decodes (synthetic SSTV frame, 98-101% link). A receive-queue size that was a third of what the TCP window needs made the Tab5 lose data when a server sent large bursts (the stock rtl_tcp sends 256 KB at a time); it is now sized to the window (256 KB bursts: 76% -> 99%).
- **Known gain on connect:** rtl_tcp keeps its tuner gain between clients, so a new stream used to inherit whatever the last client or dashboard left (FT8 about 28 dB too low while showing AUTO). Every remote stream for a new band now starts from tuner AGC.
- **Serial control:** every `RTL_TCP` / `RTL_TCPD` command except `STATUS` needs an authenticated session (extra spaces no longer slip `RTL_TCPD ENABLE` past the check).
- **Server:** off by default, binds only to a named LAN address, never all interfaces. `rtl_tcp` has no authentication; do not expose port 1234 to the internet.
- **Verified on the Tab5 against a Pi 4B running stock RTL-SDR Blog `rtl_tcp` 1.4.0:** all 19 dashboards open on the remote stream; one-hour soak; FT8 decodes (7-16 messages per slot) with and without a local dongle; remote gain changes the measured input level; recovery after a 2-minute server outage. **Not yet verified:** ACARS, SSTV and ADS-B decoding on a remote stream, frequency correction, direct sampling and offset tuning on a real signal, the gain popups on every dashboard, and audio judged by ear, and a server on a public address with no relay. The stock `rtl_tcp` on a Pi was also measured across a real cellular hop through an SSH relay: live FT8, 194 decodes in 31 slots over 8 minutes at 100% link. Changing the receiver source applies at the next stream start, not immediately.

## Unreleased — esp-rtl-sdr driver v0.9.6 — 2026-11-09

**Firmware dependency update.** The Tab5 build now pins `esp_rtl_sdr` to the published release [`v0.9.6`](https://github.com/hardcoreerik/esp-rtl-sdr/blob/v0.9.6/docs/releases/v0.9.6.md) at immutable commit `dabdda1aff6aa196f3c1523d791adf80d2c24ccc` (previously `v0.9.5`, `4bf0171`), in both `idf_component.yml` and `dependencies.lock`.

- **Driver fix carried in:** a dongle that disconnects while the receiver is starting no longer leaves the driver in FAULT or interferes with a newly attached receiver. Disconnect cleanup waits for the start attempt to unwind, and the canceled start returns `NO_DEVICE`.
- **No public API change.** The header changes only its version macro to 0.9.6. OrcSDR's existing start-failure path already handles any non-`ESP_OK` start result, so no caller change is needed.
- **Separate from the SDK USB recovery patch.** The ESP-IDF USB root-port recovery patch from PR #239 is still applied by OrcSDR's build; driver v0.9.6 does not include it.

## Unreleased source/documentation reconciliation — 2026-11-04

**Documentation update, not a new firmware release.** The current-source documentation was reconciled with the native build, implementation, dated hardware records and specific firsthand maintainer acceptance:

- **Native decoder inventory:** VHF FM RDS, ADS-B/Mode S, P25 Phase I and partial Phase II signaling, LoRa/Meshtastic, POCSAG, FT8, FT4, JS8 Normal backend, 14-mode SSTV, ACARS and VDL2; WWV timing-tick detector listed separately. No claim of identical live-RF acceptance across modes.
- **Shortwave and Airband:** working AM/USB/LSB HF reception and intelligible aviation AM voice were confirmed by the maintainer; calibration/band-wide quality remains a different scope.
- **LoRa / Meshtastic:** live unencrypted messages, telemetry, node identity and GPS decode. Encrypted traffic is labeled `ENCRYPTED FRAME`, not decrypted.
- **SSTV:** all supported 14 modes operational per maintainer; recorded conducted four-dongle image/SD tests specifically cover Robot 36, PD120, Scottie S1.
- **ACARS / VDL2:** live 8DPSK/AVLC decoding and aircraft message interpretation, including documented 213 verified frames and 135 ACARS messages in a capture; some airline-specific formats remain unparsed.
- **Receiver coverage:** Blog V4, V3c, V4L and Nooelec NESDR SMArt V5 fully supported. E4000/XTR driver is implemented and works within its native RF range in OrcSDR; the application correctly rejects requests to tune outside the supported band.
- **Dead Air / Unit ENV III:** real receiver measurements and Tab5 sensor temperature/humidity/pressure. VEIL-26 produces reproducible **symbolic interpretations** of real measured values, not scientifically validated paranormal measurements.
- **Roadmap and phase ledger:** remaining work is no longer misrepresented as unimplemented core features. Historical milestones remain date-scoped.
- **Documentation terminology:** implementation, live acceptance, repeatable RF/calibration tests and release-image acceptance are independent claims.

**No firmware code change or new physical device test is implied by this documentation entry.** A feature integrated into current source is not guaranteed to be present in an older published installer image.
