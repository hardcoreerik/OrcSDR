# OrcSDR v0.2.0-beta.6 Multi-Dongle RC2

RC2 is an experimental test release. It is not the next stable OrcSDR release,
and the related OrcSDR and driver pull requests remain unmerged.

## Receiver Support

- RTL-SDR Blog V4 remains the tested compatibility baseline.
- RTL-SDR Blog V3 support remains provisional. RC1 users reported an insertion
  reboot loop; RC2 includes USB probe and hotplug stabilization that needs to be
  tested on physical V3 hardware.
- Nooelec NESDR SMArt V5 support remains provisional. RC1 correctly identified
  and streamed from the device, but a tester received static rather than a known
  FM station and later saw the dongle disconnect from USB.
- Frequencies below 24 MHz remain blocked when the detected receiver does not
  report the Blog V4 HF-upconverter capability.
- Gain, automatic gain, RTL AGC, and Bias-T controls follow the capabilities
  reported for the attached receiver.

## Changes Since RC1

- Stabilized driver hotplug probing and device-state cleanup.
- Show the detected receiver profile instead of always displaying RTL-SDR V4.
- Reject unsupported tuning requests without reporting false success or
  cancelling P25 automation.
- Allow AM scans to finish normally with zero through six displayed candidates.
- Add visible catalog download progress, safer catalog task memory use, and
  reliable radio restart after data installation.
- Add ADS-B gain controls and clearer decoder diagnostics.
- Improve LoRa capture selection, overlapping capture/decode behavior, detector
  relearning, Record IQ states, and serial diagnostics.
- Document a deferred continuous LoRa capture queue for future work.

## Hardware Evidence

The Blog V4 driver regression passed with continuing IQ data through manual
gain, automatic gain, and RTL AGC changes, with zero driver overruns and zero
driver drops. Bias-T was kept off for this test.

ADS-B reception was confirmed with a small dipole cut for 1090 MHz, including a
live aircraft that matched nearby traffic. The MLA-30+ result is not ADS-B
acceptance evidence because that antenna is intended for AM and shortwave.

LoRa was exercised at 906.875 MHz, SF11, and BW250k. Controlled messages and
natural telemetry decoded more reliably, including consecutive valid CRCs and
capture continuing while an earlier packet decoded. One failed packet still
held the decoder for about 47 seconds, so intermittent LoRa backlog remains a
known limitation. LoRa results should be repeated with a suitable 915 MHz
antenna; reception seen through the MLA-30+ is encouraging but not the formal RF
baseline.

The catalog check and FAA aircraft reinstall showed visible progress and the
radio restarted after completion. Transfer speed remains slower than desired.

USB-C and RTL-SDR unplug/replug cycles completed without a crash on the local
Blog V4 setup.

## Known Limitations

- Blog V3 and Nooelec V5 have not passed repeatable physical reception testing.
- A V3 insertion boot loop and Nooelec no-reception/USB-disconnect report came
  from RC1; RC2 needs fresh tester logs.
- LoRa may temporarily fall behind after a difficult CRC-failed capture.
- The separate C6 firmware status/update problem reported in OrcSDR issue #82
  is not fixed by RC2.
- Airband AM, Web UI audio muting, and Web UI audio quality are separate work.
- Build success is not hardware acceptance for an untested receiver.

## Tester Requests

For Blog V3 or Nooelec V5, begin from a cold boot and test a known strong FM,
VHF, or UHF signal at 24 MHz or above. Report the exact receiver, antenna,
frequency, installation method, waterfall activity, audio result, and whether
unplug/replug works. Attach a serial log containing `RTL_STATUS`,
`RTL_DRIVER STATUS`, and `RTL_HEALTH` when possible.

For LoRa, report the region, frequency, spreading factor, bandwidth, antenna,
and how many numbered messages another Meshtastic receiver accepted versus how
many OrcSDR displayed.
