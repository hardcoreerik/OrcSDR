## 1. What this is

OrcSDR (M5Stack Tab5, ESP32-P4) now takes a PlutoSDR (Analog Devices AD9361, USB, libiio) as a second class of local receiver next to RTL-SDR
dongles. The device logic is a standalone ESP-IDF component, **esp-pluto-sdr** (`github.com/hardcoreerik/esp-pluto-sdr`, local clone
`F:\Ai\esp-pluto-sdr`): libiio-over-USB bulk pipes, an iiod client, rate/decimation planning, CU8 IQ out, hot-plug. Receive only. It depends on
nothing in OrcSDR. OrcSDR adapts to the driver, never the reverse. The owner intends to show this to SDRplay as a driver for this class of radio on
microcontrollers: keep every claim honest (Pluto-class hardware only; nothing is claimed about SDRplay hardware).

## 5. What works (measured)

- Pluto enumerates on the Tab5 USB-A port and streams at 0.96, 2.0 and 2.4 MS/s; FM audio heard; retune works; gain works through the app.
- Hot-plug resume (replug restarts the stream). Boot sequence waits for a remembered Pluto (35 s before a 3 s rail cut; a dongle-only unit keeps 8 s and 1 s).
- Reboot check, repinned driver: 11 of 11 boots clean (0 asserts, 0 EP0 errors) on COM39 with the Pluto dual-powered (attach at about 7.5 s). Earlier on the pipelined-read change: 0 asserts in 18 observed boots (baseline before it: 2 in 10). Small samples.
- Recovery ladder (`pluto_source.cpp`): failed starts or a vanished remembered Pluto trigger a 2.5 s cut of the USB-A 5 V rail (`M5.Power.setExtOutput(false, ext_USB)` via `on_pluto_power_cycle` in `main.cpp`), backoff 45 s/2/5/10 min, 6 tries, then it clears its NVS "seen" flag and reports a manual power cycle is needed. A Pluto also powered from the PC is NOT reset by this.
- Home screen shows `PLUTO` in the header chip and footer and the real rate (checked on screen).
- `PLUTO_HEALTH` line once a minute: state, samples, drops, usb errors, ladder cycles, heap numbers, battery %, mV, charging (`vbus_mv` reads -1 on this board).

## 6. What failed or is unresolved (read this first)

### 6.1 OPEN: restarting the Pluto stream after a stop intermittently fails (reproduced 2026-11-14)

Signature in the Tab5 log: `libiio pipe open: interface 4 in 85 out 03`, then 5.5 s later `W pluto_usb: no IN completion in time; cancelling`, then
`E pluto_sdr: iiod handshake failed`, repeating, then `RTL_START ESP_ERR_TIMEOUT`, and the app never retries by itself. The Pluto side looks healthy at
that moment (UDC `configured`, `iiod` active with 3 threads all in `poll`).

Evidence:
- Stress test (harness in `docs/pluto/stress_harness.cpp.txt`, drives the app's own stop and resume path in a loop): cycles with gaps 2, 5, 10 s restarted fine in about 0.5 s; the 30 s gap cycle failed (45 s, three handshake failures). The battery was plugged in, no screenshots.
- Earlier: a resume after a roughly 60 s stop failed the same way (a roughly 40 s stop had worked), and an unexplained start failure shortly after a flash had the same signature.
- **Refuted:** the internal DMA headroom. At every restart, successful or not, `dma_largest` fell 7424 to 1088 bytes. Not the discriminator.
- **Refuted/unsupported:** screenshots as the cause (one screenshot did nothing; the failing stress run took none).
- **Not the battery:** it was at 100% and charging.

Hypotheses (none tested):
1. Regression from PR 4: `in_cancel()` in `esp-pluto-sdr/src/pluto_usb.c` halts and flushes the IN endpoint then calls `usb_host_endpoint_clear` (a CLEAR_FEATURE(ENDPOINT_HALT) on EP0) while the Pluto may still be sending, then CLOSE_PIPE. A stale endpoint or toggle state could make the next session's first IN time out.
2. Pluto-side pipe state: the driver always uses pipe 0; if iiod's pipe-0 handler is not released cleanly by CLOSE_PIPE it would time out forever while pipes 1 and 2 stay free. Test by rotating the pipe index per session (needs the second and third endpoint pairs from the configuration descriptor and `OPEN_PIPE` with that index), or by retrying on the next pipe after a handshake failure.
3. Something time-dependent on the Pluto or the host link after a long idle.

**Planned next experiment (A/B, not yet run):** run the identical stress schedule against (A) the driver before PR 4 (`44494a4`, single outstanding read, with the CLOSE_PIPE fix) and (B) `dd5fd41`. Suggested schedule: 12 cycles with gaps 30, 60, 45 s repeated, 10 s run time between cycles, and make the harness keep going after a failure (retry the resume up to 3 times and log whether the failure is transient or sticky) instead of stopping at the first one. If A survives and B fails, PR 4 introduced a regression: fix it in a branch of esp-pluto-sdr, open a PR (CodeRabbit does not review this repo automatically: it has under 10 stars; the owner allowed a manual `@coderabbitai review` comment), and merge only on the owner's word. To pin the old driver: set the lock `version:` to `44494a4a69fcabf1cf128c10dae1c6ae72039291`, drop `component_hash`, delete the managed copy, build, and check the sha in the build log.

How to run the harness: it is temporary code. Paste the function and the `xTaskCreatePinnedToCore(stress_task...)` call into `pluto_source.cpp` (before `begin()` and inside it), and re-add a hook that stops and resumes the stream through the app's flags (the removed `on_pluto_power_guard` did exactly that: stop = set `rtl_stop_requested`, clear `rtl_hotplug_resume_pending`/`rtl_restart_requested`; resume = set `rtl_hotplug_resume_pending`). Never commit it. Capture the serial port for the whole run and grep `^STRESS`.

### 6.2 The 8 h soak failed at 1 h 58 min (elapsed 7,081 s)

The Pluto vanished from USB and the LAN within 11 s, with no warning (0 errors, flat heap before). Four 2.5 s rail cuts did not revive it. Plugging in the Pluto's debug cable (PC 5 V) rebooted it and it came back. Its journal ended abruptly at uptime 7,077 s: no panic, no thermal or power message, no boot in between. The Tab5 battery read 0% in the morning. **Hypothesis (not measured):** the Pluto's draw drained the Tab5's battery faster than the PC USB port could recharge it, then the Tab5's USB-A rail collapsed. Alternatives not ruled out: Pluto hard hang, stuck in early boot. The soak logger did not log battery then; it does now. A repeat soak needs the bench power fixed first (a charger or powered dock in front of the Tab5, or the Pluto on its own wall adapter with the Tab5 cable's 5 V line cut). Note 6.1 is a separate failure that a soak would also hit.

### 6.3 Smaller open items

- Dual power makes the rail-cut recovery meaningless on the current bench (the PC keeps the Pluto powered). Keep that in mind when reading ladder results.
- The Tab5's PMIC type reads "unknown" yet level/voltage/charging read fine; `vbus_mv` reads -1. The warning has not been tested on a battery-less Tab5 or on a real run-down.
- `reset-cause` decode of the Zynq registers (`0xF8000250/258`) is not verified; `orc-diag` prints raw hex only.
- No serial commands for the Pluto yet. Proposed and not built: `PLUTO STATUS` (read only), `PLUTO POWER CYCLE <ms>` (authenticated), a guard switch. Adding an `RTL_` command breaks the refactor branch's golden route tests.
- No RX2 / dual channel; the driver streams RX1 only. No TX (locked).
- esp-rtl-sdr rejects dongles not in its VID/PID table (`identify_profile`); the owner dislikes this; no issue filed.
- A capability record that lets each screen declare what it needs (so a Pluto brings TX pages) is a design direction in `Roadmap.md`, not built. A small source policy and capability mask exist.
